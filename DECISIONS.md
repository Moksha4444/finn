# Decisions & trade-offs

Format: **Decision** · Why · What we gave up.

## Architecture
- **No Supabase; SQLite + JSON committed to the repo.** One fewer service to deploy and keep secret. Gave up: a live shared DB (not needed for a single-user prototype).
- **Static-first frontend.** The push feed and demo research pages are precomputed JSON, so they load instantly even when Render is cold. Gave up: real-time freshness (data is EOD anyway).
- **Live pull = background job + polling, with staged progress text.** A single long HTTP request would time out on a cold free-tier host. Gave up: simplicity of one request.
- **Models: cheap = `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, strong = `gemini-3.8-flash`, fallback = `gemini-3.5-flash`.** The original plan was Flash + Pro, but a new free key has no Pro model. Lite passed extraction 25/25, so the bulk work stays on the cheapest tier. Gave up: Pro-level reasoning on PvD/bull-bear for now.
- **`llm.py` is provider-agnostic** (`provider:model` per task via env). Only the Gemini adapter is built. If PvD quality is shallow on Day 3, PvD/bull-bear can move to Claude with one env line plus a small adapter. Gave up: nothing yet; we avoided building an adapter we may not need.
- **Retry then fall back.** Exponential backoff on 503/429, max 3 tries, then the fallback model, with the visible label "Verdicts by fast model (quota)". The spike hit a real 503, so this is needed, not hypothetical.
- **Results PDFs: send only the consolidated results page(s).** Use the text layer when clean, and send just those PDF pages when the text is garbled (scanned). The path used is logged per call. A full PDF was ~12k input tokens. Gave up: nothing; if page detection fails, the full PDF is the fallback.
- **Update (12 Nov, measured): results go to the LLM as PDF pages only; the text path is dropped.** `scripts/compare_paths.py` sent each of 3 real filings three ways (flash-lite, numbers checked against the PDFs by hand):

  | filing | text | PDF pages | full PDF |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | TCS (clean) | 2,434 tok · 18/18 | **827 tok · 18/18*** | 11,747 tok · 18/18 |
  | HDFCBANK (scan) | 2,750 tok · **1/15** | **839 tok · 15/15** | 13,063 tok · 15/15 |
  | BAJFINANCE (scan, NBFC) | 3,543 tok · 15/15 | **839 tok · 15/15** | 1,371 tok (2-pg file) · 9/15 |

  \*Exceptional items came back as −668 instead of 668; the card uses the absolute value. Gemini bills a PDF page at a roughly flat rate, while extracted text is padded with whitespace, so page images are ~3× cheaper than text and ~15× cheaper than the whole PDF, at ~$0.0015 per filing. They are also the only path that survives scans (text got 1/15 on HDFCBANK). The planned "garble check" was deleted: there's nothing left to route. Gave up: nothing measurable. If page-finding fails, the whole PDF is still sent.
- **Results pages are found by keyword score, not layout.** A page that has ≥3 P&L line names ("before tax", "tax expense", "interest earned"…) and is not a segment, balance-sheet or cash-flow page is a candidate; consolidated beats standalone. It worked on all 3 fixtures, including OCR text.
- ~~**PAT = total profit before minority interest**, for every company.~~ **Superseded 12 Nov, see below.**
- **PAT (12 Nov, supersedes the rule above): profit attributable to owners when the filing prints it, otherwise total PAT; the card labels it "PAT (to owners)" or "PAT".** The owners' share is the headline net profit and the basis of EPS in press releases (HDFCBANK ₹20,700 cr owners vs ₹21,537 cr total; TCS ₹13,884 cr vs ₹13,934 cr). The LLM copies both lines; Python uses the owners line only if all three columns have it, so QoQ/YoY always compare like with like. Gave up: nothing; total PAT is still extracted.
- **LLM cache key for PDF pages = source-PDF hash + page numbers** (12 Nov). PyMuPDF's page subsets aren't byte-identical across calls, so keying on them caused a cache miss (and a paid re-call) every run. The test now asserts a second extraction is a cache hit.
- **NBFC prompt fix** (12 Nov): one re-run of BAJFINANCE returned null interest income (the model put it only under revenue). The prompt now says to fill `interest_earned` / `interest_expended` even when the same numbers appear elsewhere. 3/3 fixtures pass again.
- **Prompt version = hash of the prompt file.** Editing a prompt invalidates its cache automatically. No version numbers to forget to bump.
- **Cost log uses paid-tier list prices** (flash-lite $0.30/$2.50 per 1M in/out; 3.8-flash $0.75/$3.75 introductory; 3.5-flash $1.50/$9.00), even though we run on the free tier. The writeup reports what this would cost a paying user.
- **Dependencies pinned** to the versions tested locally, so a Render rebuild can't pull an untested release.
- **One-off flag is pure Python.** If "Exceptional items" ≠ 0, the card says e.g. "Includes ₹668 cr one-off". No LLM judgment about what counts as one-off.

## Push pipeline (12 Nov)
- **No SQLite / `db.py`.** HTTP responses and LLM outputs are already cached on disk, and the app reads one exported `feed.json`. A database would only duplicate them. Gave up: ad-hoc SQL over history (not needed for a 7–30 day feed).
- **Classifier = BSE subcategory first, then subject + headline, then the cheap LLM.** BSE's free-text headline often says "financial results" on unrelated filings (investor-meet notices after results), so a subcategory that maps to exactly one category wins. Ambiguous subcategories (NSE's "Investor Meet/Con. Call Updates") fall through to the subject. Only generic subjects ("Disclosure under Regulation 30", "Outcome of Board Meeting held on…") go to the LLM, with pages 1–2 of the PDF. That was 1 of 52 filings in the 7-day run. The LLM also returns a ≤12-word headline, since those subjects are unreadable. Gave up: LLM judgement on every filing (cost, and rules are explainable).
- **Results filed under "Board Meeting" are caught by the "financial results" keyword** (HDFCBANK pattern). Tested.
- **Tier overrides by rule:** board-meeting intimations, newspaper copies and call recordings → P3 whatever their category. Results decks, press releases and shareholder letters → P2 (the results filing itself stays P1). ESOP allotments filed as "Allotment of Equity Shares" → unclassified. Gave up: a per-category tier editor.
- **Known misfire we accept:** MARUTI's note on its Japanese parent's results is P1 results (the card is correctly refused: non-rupee unit). Litigation / fund-raise sizing: see below.
- **One results card per company per window** (confirmed by the user 12 Nov). Companies file the same results 2–4 times (Result, Board Meeting outcome, quarterly report). The first filing that yields a valid card keeps P1; the rest drop to P2.
- **Results card sanity gate (Python)** (confirmed 12 Nov): all three columns must have PBT and PAT and distinct period labels, otherwise there's no card and the raw link is shown. Found on BHARTIARTL: the page finder picked a clean 2-column summary page from an 84-page investor report, the model padded a third column, and a YoY change was shown as QoQ. The page finder now also scores the SEBI statement title ("results for the quarter"), which survives bad OCR. Fixture added.
- **Mgmt-change card: profile from the filing only, cited to its page.** If the filing points to an earlier letter (HDFCBANK's new MD & CEO, ASIANPAINT's new chairman), the card says "Profile not in this filing; see intimation dated …" rather than filling it from memory. Bios sometimes exceed the 35-word target; the UI clamps them to 3 lines.
- **Market-reaction boost applies to P1/P2 only** (confirmed 12 Nov). On the first run, CDSL's 3.3× volume day boosted four routine investor-meet notices to the top. Routine paperwork doesn't explain a move, and the move still raises its own price alert. Boost = +150, so a P2 the market reacted to outranks a quiet P1.
- **yfinance bars lag.** On 12 Nov at 03:30 IST the 11 Nov bar existed for Nifty and all 20 stocks with volume but a NaN close. We drop NaN-close rows, so filings whose reaction day has no complete bar show **"Reaction pending"** (no boost, no fake one). The missing 10 Nov bar was a real holiday (Diwali Balipratipada). Partial intraday bars are also dropped before 16:00 IST. Consequence: a single 16:30 cron can't be trusted for reactions, so ingest re-ranks the whole window on every run and pending items resolve on a later run.
- **NSE fallback for announcements.** CDSL is listed only on NSE (no BSE code). NSE's announcements API answered without cookies on 12 Nov. It's also used when BSE fails for a ticker: BSE served a 200 HTML error page for IRCTC's 7-day window. `fetch` now refuses to cache responses that don't start with the expected bytes (`{`, `[`, `%PDF`), so one bad answer can't poison the cache.
- **Screener slug fallback not built.** All 20 portfolio symbols resolved on screener (incl. ETERNAL). If screener 404s, announcements still come from NSE by symbol; only the pull side would need the search fallback.

- **Sizing rules for litigation and fund-raise (12 Nov, rules only, no LLM).** Filtering is the core of the brief, and "P1 litigation" for a ₹1.6 lakh penalty is noise. Python pulls every rupee amount from the subject, headline and PDF pages 1–2 (₹/Rs/INR × crore/lakh/million/billion/raw rupees) and applies:
  - Litigation / penalties **below ₹1 cr → P3** with a note ("₹1.6 lakh at stake, below ₹1 cr"). No amount found → stays P1: unsized litigation is never hidden.
  - Fund-raise stays **P1 only for equity raises** (QIP, rights, preferential, warrants, IPO, buyback, call on partly-paid) **or a non-lender's debt ≥ ₹1,000 cr**. Lenders' NCD/CP allotments are always P3 ("Routine borrowing by a lender"); lender = screener's bank/NBFC table layout ("Financing Profit" row), not a ticker list. ESOP allotments → P3. Anything else ("Allotment" with no equity or large-debt evidence) → P3 with a note.
  - The subject decides first; the PDF only when the subject is generic. Equity keywords are checked before ESOP because a preferential-issue letter mentions ESOPs in its shareholding table (BAJFINANCE).
  - Thresholds are flat because every portfolio name is a large cap. The largest amount in the text is used, which is sometimes the authorised limit rather than this tranche, and that errs towards P1. Gave up: thresholds relative to market cap (better for small caps on live pull; next step).
  - On the 30-day pull: BHARTIARTL's DoT penalty and IRCTC's ₹10.2 lakh exchange fines → P3. 4 BAJFINANCE NCD/ESOP allotments → P3. PERSISTENT ₹600 cr CP → P3, its ₹1,500 cr NCDs stay P1. SUNPHARMA ₹7,816 cr NCDs, BAJFINANCE QIP + preferential warrants, RELIANCE's Jio IPO and POLYCAB's NCLAT appeal (unsized) stay P1. 13 sizing tests.
- **Results accuracy spot-check (12 Nov), 3 non-fixture cards hand-read against the PDF page images:** TATAPOWER Q2 (filed 11 Nov), ASIANPAINT Q2, ETERNAL Q2. Revenue, total income, finance costs, D&A, exceptional, PBT, PAT and PAT-to-owners × 3 quarters: **72/72 numbers exact**. Only one deviation: a "-" exceptional line came back null instead of 0, which is harmless.
- **EBITDA now excludes exceptional items (fix from the spot-check).** TATAPOWER's PBT included a ₹119 cr net exceptional *gain* (gain on derecognising an associate less impairments), so EBITDA built from PBT was inflated: QoQ showed +8.0% instead of +5.4%, YoY +16.6% instead of +13.7%. EBITDA = printed "profit before exceptional items and tax" + finance costs + D&A; if a filing has no such line, PBT is used and the card says so. The one-off is now PBT minus that line, computed in Python, so its sign is right: "₹119 cr one-off gain" / "₹668 cr one-off charge". This closes the old abs() issue (the LLM's sign for exceptional items varied). Other income and associates' share are still not excluded, as stated on the card. **Superseded 13 Nov: other income is now excluded (see "One number per fact").**

## Feed UI (12 Nov)
- **Next.js 16 static export, all data client-side.** The app fetches `feed.json` and re-ranks it in the browser, so changing a threshold re-orders the feed instantly with no server. Gave up: server rendering (nothing to render server-side; data is EOD).
- **`rank()` ported to TypeScript (~40 lines) and both sides run `tests/rank_cases.json`.** Found while porting: Python's `format()` rounds exact ties to even (3.25 → "3.2") but JS `toFixed` rounds up ("3.3"). The port matches Python, and a tie case was added to the shared table so the two can't drift. Gave up: nothing.
- **Alerts are recomputed in the app**, not read from the export. The exported `alerts` list is only valid at default thresholds; the app rebuilds price alerts from `days` and filing alerts from `items` with the user's thresholds/categories. Data-error alerts still come from the export.
- **Alert badge = unseen alerts.** On the very first visit, everything before the latest day counts as seen, so a new user sees only the latest day's alerts (user's rule). Opening the inbox clears the badge; the rows that were new stay marked "New" for that visit. Stored in localStorage.
- **Row layout deviates from DESIGN.md's line 2 = "fact · why ranked".** At 390px the long reasons ("Ranked high · +11.6% (+9.9% vs Nifty) · 5.8× volume") truncated both halves into mush. Rows are now: line 1 = ticker · event · P1 marker, with the market reason right-aligned (`+9.9% vs Nifty · 5.8× vol`, bold when it lifted the rank); line 2 = the key fact, filing time on the right. The full rank string is in the expanded view as "Why it's here". 7–8 rows fit on a phone screen. Gave up: the raw move on the collapsed row (shown on tap).
- **Accent is deep ink-blue, not oxblood.** Losses are muted red; an oxblood priority marker would read as "down" on every P1 row.
- **Type: Libre Franklin + Source Serif 4, self-hosted via next/font.** Franklin Gothic is the newspaper workhorse sans and has tabular figures, so number columns align. The serif is used only for the day's verdict line and page titles.
- **30-day window: ingest re-run with `--days 30`** (13 Oct–12 Nov, 297 filings) so 24h / 7d / 30d all have real data. That run cost $0.049 across 59 LLM calls (mostly new results/mgmt cards). Windows are measured back from the export time, not from "now".
- **Results period headers are parsed from 8 different label formats** seen in real filings ("30.09.2026", "30th Sep'26", "3 Months ended 30.09.2026"…). Unrecognised labels are shown as printed. Tested.
- **Not built today:** motion (later pass), filing alerts linking to the in-app row (they open the PDF), credibility chip (waits for PvD), dark mode, PWA manifest.

- **Redesign after the phone review (12 Nov): the company is the unit, not the filing.** One entry per company per window; the lead is chosen by priority (results > mgmt change > fund raise > …); results decks and outcome letters fold into the results item; a P1 that isn't the lead shows as an "Also:" line so a CEO change can't hide under results. Gave up: one-row-per-filing (still there inside folds and the routine list).
- **Human titles from the cheap model, not rules.** ≤12 words from PDF pages 1–2, numbers copied as printed; the classifier's headline is reused when it already ran; results titles are written in the app from the card's numbers (no LLM arithmetic). Any failure falls back to the subject with boilerplate stripped by rule. 98 calls, $0.044.
- **Boxed cards for "What matters" (deliberate departure from the original brief's "no boxes").** The phone review asked for an app feel; cards give the 2–6 material companies a clear tier above compact rows. Capped at 6 so a results-season month stays scannable.
- **Forced light (`color-scheme: only light`).** No dark theme yet; letting Android auto-darken broke the paper identity and hid the P1 marker.

"Investment agent" only in the manifest name (install/splash). Gave up: the old F mark and its "accent square as foot" story.

Rev/PAT sit beside a 124px sparkline instead of above a full-width one; the "Details" footer and the "+N more" footer are gone (the chevron says the card opens; the fold moved inside the expanded body). - **Credibility chip (results cards), defined:** from the cached PvD JSON, counting only measured promises. **Kept** = hit or ahead; **Missed** = miss, behind pace or moved away; **Open** = on track. Verdicts that are measured but neither (partial, moved closer, no change) and not-yet-measurable promises are left to the Research page. No chip when the three counts are all zero. Links to `/research?symbol=X`. Gave up: the earlier "Mgmt delivered 3/5" wording, which hid what the denominator was.

Gave up: dilution for filings that print neither share base.

Also removed a real demo person from both prompts' examples (they had leaked into the instructions, against hard rule 2).
- **Motion pass (emil-design-eng was not installed; done to its rules by hand):** one strong ease-out curve, all under 250ms, nothing animates from nothing. Expand/collapse by grid rows 0fr→1fr (no height measuring; closed content is `inert`), press = tint + 0.99 scale, tab crossfade through React 19.3's `<ViewTransition>` (Next routes are transitions, so no router code), sparkline clip-reveal once per company per visit, alert badge pop on count change. `prefers-reduced-motion` drops everything to 1ms and turns view transitions off. Fixed: P1 "Also" items are now full filing rows inside the opened card (they were visible but unreadable, which broke the "a P1 can't hide" promise); every percentage names its basis ("YoY", "30 days", the reaction's day, "Promises:"); 200% text no longer scrolls sideways (the card is a container query that wraps its figure rows); a filing with no human title leads with the person line, not the raw subject; 44px hit areas on small links; 11px badge; long alert error text wraps. **Kept against the critique:** one "Also" line per P1 (the user's rule; the reviewer wanted one per category with a count) and the chip at one measured promise (IRCTC "Kept 0 · Missed 1"; the reviewer suggested a minimum sample). ## Promised vs Delivered (12 Nov)
- **Results calls are picked by date arithmetic, not by trusting screener's list.** A call 1–2 months after a quarter end reports that quarter; anything else is an analyst day or investor call. When screener lists two transcripts for a quarter, the BSE copy wins (its TCS "Jul 2026" company-site link was the 2025 file). Tested on the real list.
- **Transcripts go to the cheap model as text with [p.N] markers** (they are clean text, unlike scanned results), ~14k tokens each. Every promise keeps a verbatim quote and its page; a page outside the PDF is dropped as a citation.
- **Actuals and all growth maths are Python**, from screener's quarterly table (13 quarters, mirrors the exchange results filings): YoY / QoQ %, margins in percentage points. The strong model only compares the given numbers with the target and must answer not-yet-measurable when a number it needs isn't given. The "actual" text shown is built by Python from the quarters the model says it used, so no model-written number reaches the UI.
- **Verdicts are mostly not-yet-measurable for now, and that is the honest answer.** TCS gives no numeric revenue guidance; HDFCBANK's targets are FY27 and about CASA/LDR, which screener doesn't carry. Gave up (for now): bank KPI actuals. Next step: use our own results-card extraction and later transcripts' "we delivered" statements as actuals.
- **Network drops mid-request are retried like 429/503** (a 15k-token transcript call died on a connection reset on 12 Nov).

- **No paid APIs anywhere; Groq's free gpt-oss-120b is the strong model (user's decisions, 12 Nov).** The first plan kept Gemini 3.8 Flash as the strong model with patient retries, but it stayed overloaded for 17+ minutes on one verdict. Now:
  - PvD verdicts and bull/bear: **Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b`** -> `gemini-3.8-flash` -> (live path only) `gemini-3.5-flash`. Batch runs are "patient": each strong model retried, never the fast model. Every verdict stores the model that produced it; anything but the primary carries a note.
  - Groq free-tier limits for gpt-oss-120b, read from its response headers on 12 Nov: **1,000 requests/day, 8,000 tokens/minute**. So verdicts go in batches of 4 promises with a shared evidence list capped at 30 statements (largest call so far: 4,659 tokens), and a 429 waits for Groq's `retry-after`. Of the 11 models the key offered, the reasoning LLMs were gpt-oss-120b and qwen3.8-27b; the larger one won.
  - Groq is text-only, so PDF tasks (extraction, headlines) stay on Gemini.
  - Gave up: Claude (paid); fast batch runs (a 429 means waiting a minute).
- **Headline rules are enforced in Python, not just asked for in the prompt** (found by audit, 12 Nov: 24 of 107 titles ran over 12 words; one carried a number not on the pages the model read). A title must be ≤12 words and every number in it must appear in the filing pages given to the model; one retry names the problem; otherwise the app shows the cleaned subject. Applies to the classifier's reused headline too. Tested.

- **PvD verdicts are computed in Python wherever a number exists (12 Nov, user's rule: the hero feature must not be mostly "not yet measurable").** The extractor turns each target into numbers (low/high bound, direction, fiscal period, margin basis) and copies them; it does no maths. Python then:
  - maps the period to Indian fiscal quarters (FY27 = Jun 2026 ... Mar 2027) and measures against screener's quarterly table: growth = summed quarters vs the same quarters a year earlier; margins revenue-weighted, on the basis management used (IT services guide on **EBIT** margin = operating profit - depreciation; others EBITDA);
  - finished period -> **hit / miss**; running period -> **on track / ahead / behind pace**, always with the numbers ("FY27 so far (2 of 4 quarters): ...");
  - "next few quarters" guidance from an older call is checked against the quarters since that call;
  - percentage-level targets the table can't hold (CD ratio, CASA) are checked against the newest % management reported on a later call: the model only points at the statement, Python parses the number and decides.
  - The model judges only what's left (non-numeric promises). A verdict whose evidence can't be shown becomes not-yet-measurable.
  - Questions are dropped as promises (one TCS "promise" was an analyst asking about the target).
  - Result: TCS 3 promises, 2 measured (both behind pace: EBIT margin 24.4% since the Q3 FY26 call vs 26-28%; management's own "24%" vs "exit at 25% plus"). HDFCBANK 3 promises, all still not-yet-measurable, honestly: after setting "CD ratio 85-90% by FY27" management never states the ratio again on a call (Q4 FY26: "LDR is not in focus"), and transcripts + annual reports are the only pull-side sources. ~~Next option (not built): read advances and deposits from the bank's results filing and compute the CD ratio in Python.~~ Built; see "PvD review" below.
### PvD review (12 Nov, user's decisions 1-5)
- **Results filings are primary sources on the research side too.** They are the company's own exchange filings. Bank CD ratio = advances / deposits, computed in Python from the **standalone Statement of Assets and Liabilities** in each quarter's results filing on BSE. The cheap model only copies the two lines, from that one page (or the whole PDF when the filing is a scan with no text layer, as HDFCBANK's Q4 FY26 was). Python checks the column date is the quarter end and the ratio is between 30% and 200%. HDFCBANK: 98.7% (Dec 2025) → 94.6% (Mar 2026) → 95.8% → 96.0% (Sep 2026). "90-96% in FY26" = **hit**; "85-90% for FY27" = **behind pace**. Each quarter is cited to its filing and page; links use BSE's viewer URL, because older files 404 on the direct AttachLive path. Gave up: nothing; this is 4 cheap calls per bank, cached.
- **No deadline → direction of travel, never an average.** "Inch closer to 26-28%" / "next few quarters" / long-term bands are judged on where the number went since the call that made the promise, with the quarterly path shown: `Moved away: 25.3% → 24.0% → 24.0% (Q4 FY26 → Q2 FY27) vs target ≥26%`. Verdicts: hit (in the band now) / moved closer / no change / moved away. A fiscal-year target with no year named ("exit at 25% plus") counts as no deadline. Deadline targets keep hit/miss (ended) and on track/behind pace (running), and also show the path.
- **One margin definition per company.** Each call's extraction also returns the headline margin management states for that quarter (with quote and page; Python rejects it unless the number is in its own quote). If every call states one on the same basis, that series is the company's margin, e.g. TCS "Operating margin (as reported)", ASIANPAINT "PBDIT margin". Otherwise it is computed from the results table and labelled ("EBITDA margin (computed: operating profit over sales)", or EBIT when the company guides on EBIT). Definitions are never mixed within a company. Lenders' margin is NIM: reported or nothing, because the table can't compute it.
- **Promise selection: financial and checkable first, 4-6 per company.** The prompt asks for numeric financial guidance (growth, margins, profit, capex, ratios) and at most 1 dated operational target per call, and skips strategy talk. Python dedupes across calls on the extracted numbers (metric, bounds, fiscal year), keeping the oldest mention, which has the longest track record ("$5 billion by FY31" = "$5 billion by March 2031"). It then drops soft or operational promises nothing measured and keeps 6: measured first, financial first, oldest first.
- **Rules guard the extractor (found in the first 22-ticker run, where verdicts were wrong):**

## Live research (14 Nov, Batch B: the last backend batch)
- **Background job, polled.** `POST /research/{symbol}` starts a job and returns its id at once; `GET /jobs/{id}` returns a plain stage line ("Finding filings…", "Reading Q2 FY27 transcript (2/4)…", "Checking promises…", "Building the financial snapshot…", "Writing bull & bear…"). The page shows "Waking up the research server…" until Render's free tier answers. A run takes ~2.5 min (KEI locally: 149 s). Reason: one HTTP request held open for minutes dies on mobile networks and proxies; polling survives them. Gave up: streaming (SSE): more code for the same stage line.
- **One job at a time, in a thread, no queue.** A second visitor sees "Researching X for someone else right now. Try again in a minute or two." Reason: the free AI tiers are rate-limited per minute (Groq: 8k tokens/min), so two parallel runs would slow each other into timeouts anyway. Gave up: a job queue (Redis/Celery) for a demo with one reviewer.
- **Timeout is the page's, not the job's.** After 8 min (`FINN_JOB_TIMEOUT`) the page stops waiting and says the result will be saved; the job keeps running and caches its page, so the next visit is instant. Python threads can't be killed safely, and killing would throw the paid-for work away.
- **Live jobs aren't patient.** The batch retries strong models up to 8 times and never uses the fast model; a live job retries 3 times, then walks the whole chain (cheap: Gemini flash-lite → Groq gpt-oss-20b → Gemini flash; strong: Groq gpt-oss-120b → Gemini 3.8 flash → Gemini flash), so one provider's quota day can't break it. Every section names the model that wrote it, with a note when it was a fallback.
- **Failures keep what works.** Unknown symbol (screener 404): a plain message in ~7 s, no AI calls, doesn't use the day's cap. PvD fails (quota, a parse error): the page still shows the financial snapshot, prices and sources, with "The promise check couldn't run: …" in its place; that page isn't cached as final, so the next visit tries again. No transcripts: the snapshot plus "No earnings-call transcripts were found".
- **Quota protection.** Reads only the latest 4 results-call transcripts and page-level results PDFs (unchanged from batch). Daily cap `FINN_DAILY_JOBS` = 8 runs/IST day (≈20k strong-model tokens a company against Groq's 200k free day), with a polite message pointing to the 22 researched companies. Tokens, calls, models and estimated cost per run come from the router's own log and are returned with the job (and kept in `cache/live_jobs.jsonl`).
- **Cache = Render's disk + the visitor's browser.** Render's free disk is wiped on restart, so a finished page is also kept in localStorage; a second visit from the same phone is instant even when the server is asleep. Gave up: a database (Supabase) for cross-visitor persistence; the 22 precomputed pages are static on Vercel anyway.
- **Promise selection (user, 14 Nov):** measured first, then headline metrics (revenue, margin, profit), then other financial targets. "Group order inflows" keyed as revenue had taken L&T's FY26 8.5% EBITDA target's slot; order/volume/AUM wording now never counts as headline.
- **One margin definition per chart.** For L&T, screener's operating profit gives 14.1% for Sep 2026; the results filing (revenue from operations minus operating expenses) gives 8.7%. The snapshot had put the filing's three quarters among screener's five, a fake 3-4 pp sawtooth. When the two disagree by more than 1 pp on a quarter, the margin uses only the quarters read from the filings, and the label says so. Every margin label now states its computation ("revenue minus operating expenses, over revenue; other income excluded", "as management reported it on each call"). Gave up: L&T's older quarters on the chart (would need their filings read: more calls).
- **90 s per LLM call, then the next model (user, 14 Nov).** A timeout is "slow", not "down": it skips to the next model instead of three more waits on the same one. The bank test (FEDERALBNK) took 45 min because the Gemini fallback had no time limit.
- **The page before bull/bear (user, 14 Nov).** A live run saves the page as soon as the promise check and snapshot are done; bull/bear (the slowest step, the strong model) is added when written. Meanwhile the page says "Writing bull & bear…"; if it can't finish (all models down, or 6 min), a quiet "Bull/bear couldn't be generated right now." Never a stalled page.
- **Bull/bear: one fact said twice is dropped** (same period, same % to one decimal: KEI's "+38.5% revenue" and "38.52% net sales" on one side).

## Feed rules (13 Nov afternoon)
- **Management change is P1 only when a CEO, MD, CFO or Chairman joins or leaves.** Decided from the people the filing names (the management card the cheap model already extracts), not the subject line, which is usually "Change in Management". Re-appointments and redesignations, directors (including independent directors), a Deputy MD and senior management below the CEO are P2 with a note saying why. No card (extraction failed) keeps P1: never hide. On the 30-day feed: HDFCBANK MD & CEO, TRENT and ASIANPAINT Chairman, TATAPOWER CEO-designate stay P1; Bajaj Finance CISO re-appointment, HDFCBANK whole-time director re-appointment, KPIT independent director, CDSL SVP, IRCTC senior management, HDFCBANK deputy MD and group head go to P2.
- **Internal restructurings are P2 ("internal restructuring"), not M&A.** A transfer, merger or amalgamation with a wholly owned subsidiary, or a filing titled restructuring (L&T's business transfer to Vyoma.AI, ITC amalgamating Sproutlife, ETERNAL's restructuring). Buying or selling outside the group stays M&A P1 (TCS buying BBY, TRENT's subsidiary selling Netria).
- **A guarantee for a subsidiary is P3 housekeeping** (DIXON's US$7m guarantee for Padget), like a letter of comfort.
- **Prices never move backwards.** Complete bars are kept per ticker and fill any date a later yfinance fetch drops (the feed went from "up to 12 Nov" at 22:24 to "up to 11 Nov" at 03:55); a fresh fetch still wins for dates it has.
- **Alerts default to the last 7 days**, most severe first within a day (P1 filing, price move by size, volume by multiple, data problems last), with volume-only spikes under 3× folded behind "+N smaller volume spikes". The 30-day export stays one tap away.
- **Groq is the cheap tier's fallback** (gpt-oss-20b, its own daily cap), so a Gemini quota day can't stop headlines, classification or extraction: PDFs go as page-marked text ([p.N], so citations hold) and long list-shaped extractions (transcript guidance) are split into ~4k-token page chunks and joined. Limits: a scanned PDF with no text layer still needs Gemini; a whole transcript through Groq's free 8k tokens a minute takes several minutes.

Gave up: nothing; the local-ingest fallback wasn't needed.
